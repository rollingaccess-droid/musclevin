How to decode a 1965-1971 Pontiac GTO VIN

From 1965 through 1971, every Pontiac VIN, GTO included, has 13 characters in six parts: division, series, body style, model year, assembly plant, and production sequence number [1] [2]. The series code is what makes a GTO a GTO in the VIN, but only from 1966: before that, and again in 1972, the published tables have no separate GTO code. This guide walks through each part using the two published tables listed under Sources at the bottom, and shows where they disagree.

If you just want the answer for your car, paste the VIN into the free decoder on the home page. It runs in your browser and doesn't upload the VIN.

Characters 8-13: sequence number

The last six digits are the production sequence number. Tin Indian says numbering started at 100001 for V8 cars and at 600001 for six-cylinder cars, at every plant [1]. Rubber the Right Way only calls it a sequential production number [2]. In the example above, 157868 falls in the V8 range under that rule.

No engine code before 1972

Neither source's 1965-1971 table has an engine character [1] [2], so the VIN can't tell you which engine a GTO had. Pontiac added an engine code at character 5 in 1972 [1] [2].

1964 and 1972 VINs are different

1964. The VIN starts with an engine digit instead of a division digit: 6 for a six, 8 for a V8 (326 or 389). Then come the series (0 Tempest, 1 Tempest Custom, 2 Tempest LeMans), the year (4), a plant letter, and a sequence number that starts at 1001 at every plant [1]. There is no GTO series code [1] [2].

1972. The format changes to division (2), a series letter (D LeMans, G Luxury LeMans), two body digits, an engine letter, the year (2), a plant letter, and a six-digit sequence starting at 100001 [1]. Rubber the Right Way's 1972-1974 GTO table also lists F LeMans Sport and Y Ventura series [2]. Again, neither source has a GTO series letter for 1972.

What the VIN can't tell you

A 1965-1971 Pontiac VIN has no engine code, so it can't show a 389, 400, 455, or Ram Air engine. It can't show the Judge or other packages either; the example above is titled a Judge [3], but its VIN reads the same as any 1969 GTO hardtop from Arlington. For 1964, 1965, and 1972 the VIN doesn't separate a GTO from a Tempest or LeMans at all. A VIN also can't show whether the engine is original, or detect a swapped or re-stamped VIN plate. For those questions you need the engine block codes, the cowl tag, and factory documentation such as a Pontiac Historical Services report [1].
