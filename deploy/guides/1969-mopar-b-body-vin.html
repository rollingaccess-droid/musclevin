How to decode a 1969 Dodge and Plymouth B-body VIN

A 1969 Chrysler Corporation VIN has 13 characters in seven parts: car line, price class, body style, engine, model year, assembly plant, and sequence number [1] [2] [3]. Unlike a 1969 Chevrolet or Pontiac VIN, it includes an engine code, so the VIN alone tells you whether a car left the factory as a 383, 440, or 426 Hemi car. This guide covers the B-body intermediates (Plymouth Belvedere, Satellite, Road Runner, and GTX, and Dodge Coronet, Super Bee, and Charger) using the published tables listed under Sources at the bottom, and shows where they disagree.

If you just want the answer for your car, paste the VIN into the free decoder on the home page. It runs in your browser and doesn't upload the VIN.

Where to find it

The main VIN plate is riveted to the top of the instrument panel on the driver's side, between the windshield washer pivot and the A-pillar, and is easiest to read through the windshield from outside. The fender tag under the hood (usually on the left inner fender or the radiator support) repeats the VIN on its bottom line, and partial VINs are stamped in several other places, such as the engine and transmission [2].

R or K for a Plymouth? The sources disagree. Ultimate Muscle Car lists R for the 1969 Belvedere/Satellite [2], and the decoded example above starts with R [4]. The 440 Magnum Network and Lee Herman 1969 tables list K instead [1] [3]. MuscleVIN's decoder currently reads R and flags a K as an unknown car line, so if your 1969 Plymouth B-body VIN starts with K, compare the dash plate with the fender tag and the paperwork.

Other 1969 car lines: B Plymouth Barracuda, V Plymouth Valiant, L Dodge Dart (A-body); P Plymouth Fury, D Dodge Polara / Monaco, C Chrysler, Y Imperial (C-body) [1] [2] [3].

Characters 8-13: sequence number

What the VIN can't tell you

A 1969 Mopar VIN gives the original engine code, but it can't show whether that engine is still in the car, and it can't detect a swapped or re-stamped VIN plate. The two-letter model names come from a list of typical combinations [2], not a factory decode, and an M engine code only says special-order V8 [1] [2] [3], not which engine. To check options and confirm the car, compare the dash VIN with the fender tag and the partial VINs [2], and look for the build sheet and other factory paperwork. MuscleVIN's decoder can also cross-check the engine code on a fender tag against the VIN.
