VIN guide · 1965-1973 Ford Mustang

How to decode a 1965-1973 Mustang VIN

Before 1981, Ford VINs had 11 characters, not 17. A first-generation Mustang VIN has five parts, always in the same order: model year, assembly plant, body serial code, engine code, and unit number [1]. The body codes changed in 1967 and again in 1970, and Ford reused engine letters for different engines in different years, so a code only means something next to its model year [1]. This guide walks through each part using the published tables listed under Sources at the bottom.

If you just want the answer for your car, paste the VIN into the free decoder on the home page. It runs in your browser and doesn't upload the VIN.

Character 1: model year

The first character is the last digit of the model year, not the year the car was built [1].

A 4 is a 1974 Mustang II, which is outside this guide [1].

1964½ or 1965? Mustang production started in March 1964 and Ford titled every car as a 1965, so they all have a 5 VIN. Collectors call the cars built before the August 1964 changeover "1964½". The VIN can't say which on its own, but the engine code can help: U (170 six), F (260 V8), and D (289 4-barrel, premium fuel) were only used before August 1964, and T, C, and A replaced them. K (the 271 hp High Performance 289) spans both. The build date on the door data plate also tells you: a March to July 1964 date means a 1964½ [1].

Character 5: engine code

Horsepower is advertised gross horsepower as Ford rated it when new. Mustang Nerd doesn't list 1972 and 1973 ratings because they were SAE net and changed during the year [1]. The same letter can mean different engines in different years: C was a 289 2-barrel through 1968 and a 429 Cobra Jet in 1971 [1].

Characters 6-11: unit number

The last six digits are the consecutive unit number. In the example above, 123456 is the 23,456th car from that plant [1].

What the VIN can't tell you

The VIN body code doesn't give the trim level or seats; the door plate body code does [1]. The VIN can't tell a 1964½ from a later 1965 on its own [1], and it can't show whether the engine is still the original one. It also can't detect a swapped or re-stamped VIN. For those questions you need the door data plate, the engine and part date codes, and factory paperwork.
